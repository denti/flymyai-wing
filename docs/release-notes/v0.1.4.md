**Fixes a side effect of v0.1.2: your Mac would never idle-sleep again while Lidwing was
installed.**

### What was wrong

Lidwing keeps a Mac awake with two separate mechanisms. One stops the sleep that a lid close
triggers. The other stops the ordinary idle timer — the one that sleeps your Mac after you walk
away — and the first mechanism does not cover it.

Since v0.1.2 Lidwing turns itself on when it launches, which is the point: install it and it
works. But it also held the second mechanism from the moment you logged in, for as long as its
duration lease lasted. So a Mac with Lidwing installed **stopped sleeping on its own**, whether
or not anything was happening. Walk away with the lid open, come back to a warm laptop with a
flatter battery than it should have, for a feature you were not using.

### What it does now

The idle-sleep hold is taken only when there is a reason for it:

- **the lid is shut** — the case Lidwing exists for, and it is essential there;
- **a coding agent is running** — your run has to survive you walking away with the lid open;
- **you switched Lidwing on yourself** — you asked, so it does what it has always done.

Armed automatically with the lid open and nothing running, your Mac sleeps like any other Mac.

Closing the lid takes the hold back within milliseconds, and a five-second check catches it even
if that signal is missed — well inside any idle-sleep timeout. The lid-close protection itself is
never released while Lidwing is on.

### Also since v0.1.3

Diagnostics now include every power assertion held on your Mac, classified: which ones matter,
which Lidwing coexists with, and how many were irrelevant. That is the information that explains
why Lidwing did or did not say anything, and it is what makes a support report answerable.

**304 tests on Linux, 309 on macOS 15 and macOS 26**, zero failures, zero skipped.

### Unchanged, and still the important part

The mechanism is proven on **one** Mac for **twelve seconds**, with a positive control. No 8-hour
soak yet. Nothing on Intel, Air, fanless, T2, or any macOS other than 15.5. Lidwing tells you when
it is running on hardware nobody has tested it on.
